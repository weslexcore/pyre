# Brand voice

This file is the source of truth for **how Pyre talks**. Read it before drafting any copy: headline, eyebrow, body, caption, alt text, microcopy. For business facts (location, pricing, offerings, contacts) and visual cues, read [`business-details.md`](business-details.md).

The job of this doc is to press every line you write toward the voice that already lives on the website. If a draft sounds like a wellness brand, a LinkedIn post, a luxury spa, or an AI: rewrite.

---

## TL;DR press

Run every draft past these before you ship it.

1. **No em-dashes.** Anywhere. Use a period, a comma, a colon, a regular dash or a new sentence. This is a brand rule, not a preference.
2. **No AI tells.** No "It's not X, it's Y." No tricolon flourishes ("relax, reset, return"). No "elevate," "unlock," "journey," "unleash," "transform your life." No polished closer paragraphs that summarize what you just said.
3. **Read it aloud.** If it sounds like a LinkedIn post or a brochure, it isn't Pyre.
4. **Invite, don't prescribe.** "Come." "Meet." "Take." "Bring a friend." Not "You should." Not "Discover."
5. **Fragments are fine.** A line by itself, ending in a period, is the most Pyre thing on the page. Use them.
6. **Self-care, together.** When in doubt, return to the tagline. It's the whole brand in three words and a comma. The comma matters.
7. **Pyre is a Social Sage** (see §1). Calm, certain, and warm. Knows the practice. Doesn't lecture. Invites you in.

## 1. The Social Sage

The Pyre archetype is the **Social Sage**. The Sage knows: wisdom, expertise, calm authority, insight. Pyre takes that posture and makes it social: the wisdom is shared, the practice is communal, the bench in the sauna has room for one more.

**What this sounds like.**

- Calm and certain. Pyre does not need to convince you it works. It states what happens.
- Spoken from inside the practice, not outside it. "Most first-timers tap at thirty seconds" (from `posts/cold-plunge-for-beginners/index.html`) is Sage voice: it's a fact from someone who's watched it happen a thousand times.
- Warm and matter-of-fact at the same time. The Sage is steady. Steadiness is what makes the invitation land.
- Plain language. The Sage doesn't need the big word.

**What this is not.**

- Not the **optimization-bro Sage**. No "stack," "protocol," "biohack," "performance," "recovery." That's a different brand selling a different feeling.
- Not the **luxury-spa Sage**. No "indulge," "escape into," "sanctuary," "ritual of self-renewal." Pyre is outdoors, plain, communal, and a little raw. It is not whispering candles.
- Not the **LinkedIn thought-leader Sage**. No tricolons, no rhetorical contrasts, no "Here's what we've learned." The Sage doesn't perform wisdom; it just speaks plainly.
- Not the **guru Sage**. Pyre does not prescribe. The user runs their own ritual. (See "Autonomy of Experience" below.)

When you're stuck, picture one of the founders sitting on a sauna bench, ladle in hand, telling a first-timer what to expect. That register is the target.

## 2. Mission, values, and the duality tags

These are not slogans to drop into copy. They are the **frame** that shapes every sentence.

### Mission

> Pyre's mission is to build a new social culture, centered around the use of traditional hot and cold therapies.

The two load-bearing words are **social** and **traditional**. Pyre is not inventing a new wellness modality. It is reviving a practice as old as people, and putting people back in the room with each other.

### Core values, and how they show up in copy

| Value | Voice translation |
| --- | --- |
| **Immersion** | Use sensory verbs. *Sweat. Plunge. Breathe. Sit. Steam.* Describe what the body feels, not what the brand promises. "Body buzzing, mind clear." |
| **Connection** | Default to *together, communal, with others*. Avoid the optimized-individual frame. The reader is one of a group, even when reading alone. |
| **Autonomy of Experience** | Never prescribe. The brand offers a template; the guest chooses the rhythm. *"At your own pace." "This time is yours." "Personalize their rituals."* No "you should," "you must," "everyone needs." |

### The three duality tags

Pyre's identity system uses three recurring phrases (they appear on every page of the brand guidelines as a kind of footer chant):

- **A Duality.** The hot/cold pairing, and the inner/outer pairing. Use when you want to point at the both-and structure of the practice.
- **Something Social.** The part that separates Pyre from a solo plunge tub. Use when the post is about the room, the bench, the table after the session.
- **From Purge to Plunge.** The arc of a session. Use as a closer, a poster line, a story arc title.

You don't need all three in any single post. Use one when it lands. Never invent a fourth.

## 3. Voice anchors

### Posture

Calm. Grounded. Declarative. Inviting, not selling. Pyre states what is and what's offered, and lets the reader come to it. There is no urgency play, no scarcity panic, no exclamation point selling.

### Sentence rhythm

Short. Often a fragment. Then sometimes one slightly longer line that gives the idea room. Comma splices are fine when they slow the reader down. Periods do more work than exclamation points. White space is part of the voice.

A working pattern, used a lot on canvas:

> Hot water. Cold water. Eye contact.
> Conversation without notifications.
>
> (`posts/loneliness-crisis/index.html`)

Three fragments, then one slightly longer line. That's the shape.

### Capitalization

- **Sentence case** is the default for headlines and body. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns; everything else stays lower.
- **ALL CAPS** is a typographic choice in display, not a voice choice. Reserve for short eyebrows or poster lines where the design calls for it. Don't all-caps a paragraph.
- **Title Case** is rarely right. Avoid it in headlines.
- `PYRE` renders as a wordmark. Don't write "the PYRE experience" mid-sentence; write "Pyre."
- "Sauna Master" capitalizes when used as a credential.

### Punctuation

- **No em-dashes.** This is the rule. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or a new sentence.
- **Periods over exclamation points.** Pyre is certain, not excited.
- **Commas can slow the reader.** "Self-care, together." The comma is the whole tagline.
- **Colons introduce lists, lowercase after.** "Curated experiences blending: sauna, cold plunge, breathwork, movement."
- **Serial comma**, yes.
- **No ellipses** to imply emotion. They read as evasive.
- **No "It's not X, it's Y" constructions.** This is the single most reliable AI tell. If you write one, rewrite it.

### Point of view

Second person, mostly imperative. *Come. Take. Bring. Meet. Sit.* When the brand speaks about itself, it's "we" sparingly, more often the practice itself ("the practice," "the cold," "the sauna").

## 4. Words to use, words to avoid

### Use

Restored. Reconnect. Together. Practice. Ritual. Communal. Heat and cold. Sweat. Plunge. Reset. Steam. Self-care. Grounded. Bathhouse. Bench. Breath. Round. Pace. Quiet.

Plus the canon phrases below.

### Avoid

Wellness optimization. Biohacking. Hack. Stack. Protocol. Routine. Performance. Recovery (as a brand noun). Therapy session. Modality. Synergy.

Journey. Unlock. Transform your life. Unleash. Elevate. Game-changer. Discover. Immerse yourself (the brand, not the practice, is doing the immersing). Indulge. Sanctuary. Oasis. Restorative experience (as a stock noun).

Curated. Crafted. Bespoke. Premier. World-class. Best-in-class.

If a word would feel at home in a hotel-spa brochure or a LinkedIn post, it doesn't belong on a Pyre slide.

## 8. Surface notes

Posts in `apps/social/posts/<slug>/index.html` have a small set of text surfaces. Each one has its own register.

### Eyebrow

Topic header above the headline. Often ALL CAPS in display (the design decides). Two to four words. A topic label, not a sentence. Examples from real posts:

- `Cold plunge 101` (`posts/cold-plunge-for-beginners/index.html:22`)
- `How cold` (line 51)
- `You're not alone` (line 199)
- `Self care together` (line 228)
- `Loneliness` (used as a series tag across all five `loneliness-crisis` pages)

### Display headline

The big line. Two to seven words is the common length. Often broken across two or three lines, with a single colored **accent word** carrying the weight (sky, red, gold). Always pair the headline with a short body line below it.

- "Your first **plunge**." (cold-plunge-for-beginners, page 1)
- "When did being **alone** become the default?" (loneliness-crisis, page 1)
- "Self care, **together**." (loneliness-crisis, page 5)

### Body lede

One or two short sentences. Almost always two lines: a fragment, then a closer. Never a paragraph.

- "Here's what actually happens. / And what to do." (cold-plunge-for-beginners, page 1)
- "Hot water. Cold water. Eye contact. / Conversation without notifications." (loneliness-crisis, page 4)
- "The empty room is the modern condition." (loneliness-crisis, page 1)

### Stat line

Number plus one short label. The number is the hero; the label is plain.

- `39–50°F / Our plunge temperature` (cold-plunge-for-beginners, page 2)
- `1 in 2 / American adults report loneliness.` (loneliness-crisis, page 2)

### List item (tips, outcomes)

Imperative, short. The fewer words the better. Bold sub-strings allowed for the action verb.

- "Breathe slow. **4 in, 6 out.** Exhale longer than inhale." (page 4)
- "Decreases anxiety. Lowers stress. Deeper sleep." (page 5)

### Footer cite

The smallest type. Either a source attribution or a tiny brand mark. Use one of:

- `Pyre · Richmond, VA`
- `Pyre · <topic>` (e.g., `Pyre · cold plunge basics`)
- `Pyre · self care together`
- `<Author> <Year>` for cited research (e.g., `Šrámek 2000 · Buijze 2016`)
- `pyresauna.com · Richmond, VA` for the CTA poster page

Period-free; the middle dot does the separator work.

### Caption (Instagram, written off-canvas)

Slightly more conversational than on-canvas copy, but the same rules apply. Lead with a fragment. Avoid emoji unless the post is about an event. End with a soft invitation, not a hard CTA. Sign off with the tagline, the location, or one of the duality tags. No hashtag soup; one to three tags max, lowercase.

## 9. The press (final check)

Before shipping any draft, run it past these five questions. If you can't answer "yes" to all of them, rewrite.

1. **The LinkedIn test.** If you put this line in a LinkedIn post, would anyone notice? If yes, rewrite.
2. **The invitation test.** Does the copy invite ("come," "meet," "take," "sit"), or does it prescribe ("you should," "you need to," "discover")?
3. **The AI-tell test.** Any em-dashes? Any "It's not X, it's Y"? Any "elevate / unlock / journey / transform / curated / premier"? Any closing summary paragraph? Any tricolon flourish?
4. **The fragment test.** Is at least one line a fragment? Pyre rhythm leans on them.
5. **The Sage test.** Would the founder, on a sauna bench, ladle in hand, say this out loud to a first-timer? If it sounds like a brochure or a coach instead, it isn't there yet.

## When in doubt

If a phrase, fact, or claim isn't covered here, default to the live site (`apps/landing-page/src/lib/`) and to `business-details.md`. Don't invent product names, credentials, or numbers. The brand's strength is plainness; reaching for invention is usually where copy starts to drift.
