## The Inspiration
Last winter, I sat in a wood-fired sauna by the Rivanna River in Charlottesville. It was my best friend's birthday. My daughter had just turned one a few months before.

Inside the warmth of that sauna, surrounded by strangers with different stories and backgrounds, something shifted. We talked about everything and nothing—passion, purpose, the tension of modern life. No one had their phone. No one was trying to sell anything. For the first time in a long while, **I felt human again.**

It hit me just how *starved* I had been for that kind of connection.

Becoming a parent during COVID had slowly thinned my social world. Relationships that once felt rich had become fragmented—reduced to text threads, memes, and the occasional video call. I'd been keeping up with everything: the news, the apps, the texts… but somewhere along the way, I'd stopped keeping up with myself. With the people in front of me. With the world right outside my door.

That day in the sauna, I remembered what it feels like to be **present—not productive**. To be **curious—not curated**. To be warm, quiet, and still enough to hear what my body and heart had been trying to say.

And I knew: Richmond needed a place like this.

## Reclaiming the Third Space

That's why we are building Pyre—**Richmond's first bathhouse in over a century**. A tech-free third space that exists outside of work and home. A space where you don't have to *do* anything… except *be*.

At Pyre, you'll find contrast therapy—sauna and cold plunge—as well as guided breathwork, meditation, yoga, and sound baths. But more than that, you'll find something many of us have forgotten how to look for: **true presence, and real connection**.

No notifications. No algorithm. No performance.

Just sensation. Just breath. Just each other.

## A Place to Be Human

We're living in an era where we're more connected than ever, and yet, many of us feel **more isolated and disoriented than we've ever been**.

We're disconnected from our food. From our bodies. From our neighbors and coworkers. From the natural rhythms of life that used to anchor us. Even joy feels like another thing to schedule, document, and optimize.

But when we reconnect—*really* reconnect—with the world around us, something in us wakes up. We remember how to feel. How to be a father, a friend, a husband… not just in name, but in depth. We remember what it means to sit beside someone, shoulder to shoulder, in silence or in story, and feel safe. Seen. Part of something.

That's the gift Pyre hopes to offer: **a place to come back to yourself, and to each other**.

## A Space for All

In the past, shared experiences were easier to come by. We grew food together. We raised children in community. We told stories around literal fires.

Today, everything is so fragmented. Our lives are so hyper-curated that we rarely bump up against people different from us. And yet, it's in those encounters—those unexpected overlaps—that we grow. That we soften. That we remember how big and beautiful the human experience really is.

At Pyre, you won't find curated sameness. You'll find diversity—of culture, of thought, of experience. That's intentional. Because **healing doesn't happen in a vacuum. It happens in community.**

## An Invitation

I was born and raised here in Richmond. This city is in my bones. And I want Pyre to be a gift to the community that raised me—a sanctuary from the noise. **A place to remember what it feels like to be human.**

So I invite you: come warm your bones. Come cool your nervous system. Come reconnect—with yourself, with others, with life.

We're building something ancient, something modern, and something *deeply needed*. We're building Pyre. 

**We can’t wait to welcome you in.**
